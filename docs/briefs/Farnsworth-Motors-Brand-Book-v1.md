# Farnsworth Motors — Brand & Marketing Book v1

*Status: working draft, September 2026. This is the document you hand to any AI or human before they touch the brand. Everything else (website, 3D, ads, listings) derives from it.*

---

## 1. What Farnsworth Motors is

Farnsworth Motors is a Utah-licensed dealership that sells rebuilt-title vehicles it repaired itself. That last part is the whole business. Most rebuilt-title lots buy cars that someone else fixed somewhere, mark them up, and hope the buyer doesn't look too closely. Farnsworth is the opposite: the dealership, the body shop, and the mechanical shop are one operation under one name. The same people who choose the car choose the parts, do the repair, and put it on the lot. Parts are OEM. The shop already does body work for franchise-level brands (BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Toyota and others), so the work on a Farnsworth car is the same work those brands trust.

The one-line version: **Rebuilt right. Shown completely.**

The plain-English version: *We only take cars with minor, cosmetic damage — no frame hits, no mechanical problems — repair them in our own shop with OEM parts, and show you everything we did.*

## 2. Company structure

Farnsworth is an umbrella. Three entities, one standard:

| Entity | Role | Public-facing? |
|---|---|---|
| **Farnsworth Motors** | Dealership. Sells the finished vehicle. The consumer brand. | Yes — primary |
| **Farnsworth Collision** *(working name)* | Autobody and paint. Repairs Farnsworth inventory and does B2B work for premier brands. | Yes — secondary, credibility layer |
| **Farnsworth Service** *(working name)* | Mechanical inspection and repair. Certifies that nothing mechanical was touched or, if it was, that it was done right. | Yes — secondary, credibility layer |

Rule for the brand: the dealership sells, the shops *prove*. On the website the shops appear as "the reason you can trust the car," not as separate businesses competing for attention. Sub-entity names above are placeholders — decide the real names before the site copy is written (see open questions).

## 3. The Farnsworth Standard (what we do and refuse to do)

This is the substance behind the marketing. Every visual and every sentence should be traceable to one of these.

1. **Selection.** We buy vehicles with minor, mostly cosmetic damage. No structural or frame damage. No flood. No fire. No damage that required mechanical repair to core systems. If it would cause problems later, we don't buy it.
2. **Repair.** All work is done in-house by the same shop that does body work for franchise brands. No subcontracting the important parts.
3. **Parts.** OEM. Not aftermarket, not "OEM-equivalent."
4. **Disclosure.** Every car comes with its rebuild record: what was damaged, what was replaced, what was inspected. Utah requires rebuilt-title disclosure; we go past the requirement and make the disclosure the selling point.
5. **Condition.** The cars are late-model, well maintained, and finished to the standard of the brand that built them.

## 4. Who we sell to

**The informed value buyer.** They already know a rebuilt title means 20–40% off a comparable clean-title car. They're not scared of the concept; they're scared of the *execution*. They've heard the horror stories (bent frames, airbag lights taped over, Marketplace flippers) and they want the one lot that isn't that.

What they're asking, in order:

1. How bad was the damage, really?
2. Who fixed it and with what?
3. Will this cause problems in two years?
4. Can I finance and insure it?
5. Why should I believe you?

Farnsworth's job is to answer 1–3 *before* they ask, visually, on the first screen.

Secondary audiences: families needing a second vehicle at a real price; buyers stepping into a premium brand (BMW, Mercedes, Lexus) they couldn't afford clean-title; franchise body-shop clients (B2B, served by the Collision entity).

## 5. Positioning

**Category:** rebuilt-title vehicles.
**Frame:** the trustworthy, sophisticated end of that category — the Porsche/Apple version of a space full of AutoSavvy-style volume lots and Facebook flippers.

Positioning statement (internal):

> For informed buyers who know a rebuilt title is a good deal but don't trust the people selling them, Farnsworth Motors is the dealership that repairs its own cars in a shop premier brands already trust, using OEM parts, on vehicles it selected for minor cosmetic damage only — and shows you the entire rebuild instead of hiding it.

**Who we are not** (visual and verbal anti-references):

- The big-box volume rebuilt chain (warehouse of cars, price stickers as the design, "no-haggle" as the personality). AutoSavvy is the obvious reference in Utah. We are smaller, slower, and more selective, and the brand should feel that way.
- The traditional Utah rebuilt dealer with a 2012-era website, stock photos of handshakes, and a "we've been in business since…" paragraph as the entire trust argument (the Prestman / Summit tier).
- The buy-here-pay-here lot and the Marketplace flipper: balloons, "$500 DOWN," a car photographed in a driveway.

If a design decision would make Farnsworth look like any of those, it's wrong.

## 6. Messaging pillars

Every piece of marketing hits at least one; the homepage hits all three.

**Pillar 1 — Chosen, not scavenged.**
We reject most cars. Cosmetic damage only. Message: *the damage was skin-deep; the frame and the mechanicals were never touched.*
Proof: the intact structure, shown. The rebuild record listing what was and wasn't affected.

**Pillar 2 — Rebuilt by the shop the brands use.**
In-house body and mechanical. OEM parts. Message: *the same hands and parts that fix a Mercedes at the Mercedes standard fixed this car.*
Proof: the Collision and Service entities, the OEM part, the process.

**Pillar 3 — Shown completely.**
Nothing hidden. Message: *you'll see more of this car than you'd see of a clean-title car at another lot.*
Proof: before/during/after imagery, the rebuild record on every listing, the disclosure made beautiful instead of buried.

## 7. Voice

Farnsworth sounds like a precise, calm engineer who's proud of the work and has nothing to hide. Not a salesman. Not a buddy. Not a luxury brand whispering about "craftsmanship."

**Principles**

- Say the specific thing. "Rear quarter panel replaced, OEM, refinished in-house" beats "professionally repaired."
- Short sentences. Nouns and verbs. Cut adjectives that aren't measurable.
- Lead with the disclosure, then the deal. Never the reverse.
- Never defensive about rebuilt titles. We don't argue that rebuilt is fine; we show that *this* rebuild is right.
- Plain English over shop jargon, but use the real terms when they carry proof (OEM, unibody, panel gap, paint depth).

**Words we use:** rebuilt, rebuild record, cosmetic, structural, OEM, in-house, inspected, disclosed, selected.
**Words we don't:** salvage (as a selling word), "like new," "mint," "flawless," "as-is" (legally required in places, never as marketing), "cheap," "deal of the week," "no haggle," "certified" (unless a real certification program exists).

**Sample lines**

- Rebuilt right. Shown completely.
- Cosmetic damage. OEM parts. Our shop. Every step on record.
- We turned down eleven cars to buy this one.
- The frame was never touched. Here's how we know.
- The same shop that repairs BMWs for BMW repaired this BMW.

## 8. Visual identity

**Color**

| Role | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ground (light) | #F5F4F1 warm white | Default page and studio background |
| Ground (dark) | #0E0E0F near-black | Hero and immersive sections |
| Ink | #1A1A1A | Body text |
| Steel | #8E8E8A | Secondary text, hairlines, primer/bare-metal reference |
| Oxide red | #B33A26 | **Only** for: damage/attention markers in 3D and diagrams, primary CTA, one accent per screen. Never a car color. Never a background. |
| Intact green *(new)* | #2F6B4F | The single color that means "untouched / verified" in diagrams and the 3D reveal. Used sparingly, always next to a label. |

The red/green pair is functional, not decorative: red = what was damaged and replaced, green = what was verified untouched. Once a buyer learns that on the homepage, every listing reads instantly.

**Type**

- Bitter (serif) — headlines. The weight and warmth of something established.
- Archivo (sans) — UI, body, listings.
- IBM Plex Mono — data: VINs, part numbers, measurements, rebuild-record entries. Mono is the "record" voice; anything in mono is a fact.

**Imagery and 3D principles** (the "Apple brand design" answer to questions 10–12)

- One object, one light, empty space. The car sits alone in a studio; nothing else in frame.
- Two worlds only: warm white studio (Pillar 1 and 3, listings) and near-black studio (hero, Pillar 2). Never a garage set, never a lot, never a sunset.
- Materials are honest: satin paint, matte primer, bare aluminum, black rubber. No chrome flare, no lens flare, no glossy floor reflections beyond a soft contact shadow.
- Damage is shown as a quiet fact, not drama. A dented quarter panel, not a wreck.
- Motion is slow and mechanical: panels seat into place, a cut-plane sweeps, the camera drifts. Nothing bounces.
- Red and green are the only saturated colors that ever appear in an image.
- Real inventory photography (when it starts) follows the same rules: white sweep or dark studio, one car, no props.

**Signature motif — The Reveal**

A clipping plane sweeps through the vehicle, hiding the body panels to show the structure beneath. Structure that was never touched glows intact green. Panels that were replaced are outlined in oxide red with a mono label ("RH quarter panel — OEM — replaced"). This is the brand in one image: shown completely.

## 9. Legal and disclosure guardrails

- Every vehicle is described as *rebuilt* or *rebuilt/restored title* in the listing title, not buried in fine print. Utah brands these titles and requires dealer disclosure; treating that disclosure as a feature is both compliant and on-brand.
- Never state or imply "like new," "no damage," or that a rebuilt title carries no downside (insurance and resale differ; say so when asked).
- Never use another brand's logo, badge, or trademarked design as Farnsworth's own identity (see the Codex handoff on the hero vehicle).
- "OEM" means OEM. If a part ever isn't, the record says so.

## 10. Open questions (decide before the site copy is final)

1. Real names for the body shop and mechanical shop entities.
2. Do the shops take retail customers, or only Farnsworth inventory and B2B?
3. Is there a warranty or inspection guarantee we can name? If yes it becomes a pillar; if no, don't imply one.
4. Financing partners who accept rebuilt titles — this is the #4 buyer question and needs a real answer on the site.
5. Location and service area language (Utah statewide? Wasatch Front?).
